# Facts raw: 7 rulers killed by food (fact-check notes)
Confidence tags: [SOLID] [DISPUTED] [LEGEND]. "(memory)" = from general knowledge, not re-verified online.

## 1. Claudius, d. 13 Oct AD 54, Rome
- Death date/place SOLID (Wikipedia infobox). Aged 63. Nero succeeded.
- Ancient sources: Tacitus Annals 12.66-67 (taster Halotus served poisoned mushrooms; poisoner Locusta supplied poison; doctor Xenophon finished him with poisoned feather down throat) (memory+search). Suetonius (Claudius 44-45) gives several versions, admits sources disagree on who/where. Cassius Dio: Agrippina got poison from Locusta. Pliny the Elder NH 22: mushrooms as "exquisite" weapon, Agrippina via Claudius (secondary cite). Juvenal also mentions it.
- Mushroom type: NOT stated precisely. Popular "Amanita caesarea" (Caesar's mushroom, prized) or death cap Amanita phalloides = modern guesses. Classical Quarterly paper argues death cap, maybe accident.
- Doubts: Ferrero (1911) called it absurd; Bagnani (1946) heart attack; Seneca's satire blames fever. Agrippina poisoning = DISPUTED, mushroom meal plausible, murder unproven.
- Vivid: taster Halotus in attendance; feather dipped in poison; Agrippina's son Nero succeeded; Nero later joked mushrooms were "food of the gods" (Suetonius, memory).

## 2. Attila, 453
- Priscus (eyewitness-era, lost) -> via Jordanes Getica (c. 551, abridging Cassiodorus). Wedding to Ildico (Germanic girl), feast, drinking, went to bed; found dead next morning, no wound, blood. Jordanes: lay on his back in stupor, nosebleed, blood flowed down throat and choked him.
- Date: 453 (early; exact day unknown). Place: his camp beyond Danube/Pannonia.
- Alternatives: oesophageal varices rupture (alcohol), brain haemorrhage; Marcellinus Comes (~80 yrs later) says stabbed by wife = most historians reject.
- Solid: died at wedding night after feast, no wound. Disputed: cause. Not "food" strictly: wine/overeating.
- Vivid: found next morning, servants broke in; silk tent mourning, men cut hair/gashed faces; buried in three coffins gold/silver/iron, gravediggers killed (Jordanes, legend-like).
- After: sons fought, Huns beaten at Nedao 454.

## 3. Henry I of England, d. 1 Dec 1135
- Fell ill at Lyons-la-Forêt (Normandy) after hunting, Nov 1135; died 1 Dec after ~a week.
- Henry of Huntingdon: "surfeit" of lampreys eaten against physician's advice. Dish/city: NOT specified in sources I found; "lampreys" only.
- Modern: improbable as cause; Listeria suggested (Wikipedia). Lampreys were his favourite (memory).
- Aftermath: confession, debts settled; body embalmed at Rouen, buried Reading Abbey (his foundation); nephew Stephen took crown -> Anarchy (civil war with Matilda).
- Memory-level, unverified: body decayed horribly, brain/entrails put in jars; the embalmer died from the stench. Treat as legend.

## 4. Pope Paul II, d. 26 July 1471, Rome (aged 54)
- Solid: sudden death 26 July 1471.
- Melons: contemporary doctor blamed excess "humid foods"; reportedly two large melons the day before (search). Wikipedia: "heart attack"/indigestion after excess melon; also anti-papal rumour of death during sex with a page boy (Infessura-type hostile sources).
- Platina: imprisoned by Paul II 1468 (tortured), wrote hostile Lives of the Popes (pub. Venice 1479). NOT verified that Platina is source of the melon claim; he did attack Paul. Stroke/apoplexy = common modern diagnosis, not primary-source verified.
- Vivid: Paul II planned lavish papal tiara with jewels (memory); Carnival races on Via Lata.

## 5. Charles VI, Holy Roman Emperor, d. 20 Oct 1740 (age 55)
- Fell ill at Favorita palace Vienna after hunting at Halbturn (Hungary) (Carlyle via search); died at Hofburg.
- Voltaire (Memoirs; also Siècle de Louis XV): death by "indigestion occasioned by eating champignons." Wikipedia footnote: Catalan-style mushroom stew, cook, chilly day duck hunting; "death cap" claim is Voltaire-attributed, not proven.
- Alternative: chill/cold, gout, pleurisy; also stew "of ceps"? NOT verified.
- Vivid/solid: no sons; Pragmatic Sanction 1713 for daughter Maria Theresa; death triggered War of the Austrian Succession (1740-48), Frederick II seized Silesia in Dec 1740; last male Habsburg. Frederick of Prussia: Carlyle quotes.
- Famous Voltaire line, if wanted: "this mushroom dish changed the destiny of Europe" (paraphrase, attributed; verify exact wording before quoting).

## 6. Adolf Frederick of Sweden, d. 12 Feb 1771 (Shrove Tuesday, "fettisdagen")
- Solid: died suddenly Stockholm 12 Feb 1771 aged 60; Livrustkammaren: doctors said stroke; Wikipedia: heart failure or poisoning-like symptoms.
- Legend: huge meal: lobster, caviar, sauerkraut, kippers, champagne, then 14 helpings of hetvägg (semla in hot milk). Rests on weak/late sources (2007 The Local, semla bakery site). Often traced to Count Oxenstierna's diary: unverified. Snopes: not corroborated; palace records missing. Number 14 grew in retelling. Wikipedia: modern writers call it "propaganda" (c.f. Rainer 2021).
- Safe: dinner on Shrove Tuesday incl. semla plausible; "ate himself to death" NOT proven. Nickname "semla king" popular tale.
- Vivid: semla-in-milk = hetvägg; his wife Louisa Ulrika (Frederick the Great's sister) and rule limited by parliament (memory).

## 7. Zachary Taylor, d. 9 July 1850, White House
- 4 July 1850: Independence Day ceremonies at Washington Monument site, hot day; afterwards ate cherries and iced milk (popular account; some say also pickles - unverified); fell ill that night.
- Died 9 July (about 5 days later) aged 65; 16 months in office; doctors: "cholera morbus"/acute gastroenteritis. Fillmore succeeded.
- 1991: exhumed 17 June 1991 after Clara Rising's arsenic theory; Kentucky chief medical examiner George Nichols: arsenic trace far too low, natural causes; family accepted. Likely: gastroenteritis (bacterial; typhoid/cholera/dysentery suggested; Washington sanitation poor, sewer near White House). Parenti dissent = minority.
- Vivid: Washington's sewage canal/open drains near White House; his horse "Old Whitey" followed funeral (memory).

- Paul II: Platina as proven source of melon story; exact stroke diagnosis; "two melons" as certain.
