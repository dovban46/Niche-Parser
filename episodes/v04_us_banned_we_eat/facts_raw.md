### 1. Kinder Surprise (egg with toy inside)
- Law: Federal Food, Drug & Cosmetic Act 1938, sec. 402(d)(1): confectionery with non-nutritive object "imbedded" is adulterated unless FDA recognises functional value. [SOLID]
- Enforcement: FDA Import Alert 34-02 (original 2008, revised 2024): detention without exam of Kinder Surprise from all shippers. [SOLID]
- Official reason: choking hazard (FDA import alert; CPSC also found toy a choking hazard). [SOLID]
- Allowed: Kinder Joy (toy in separate compartment, not embedded). Only a reasoned distinction, no FDA statement "Joy is lawful". [DISPUTED] Kinder Surprise still legal in US only as "no" - not sold.
- Story: CBP cited 3,000+ seizures of Kinder eggs since 2010 (Army/CBP notice 2014); travellers/mailers fined. [SOLID] "60,000 seized in 2011" figure: single commentary [DISPUTED - skip number].
- Caveat: Not a "ban on chocolate"; it is the toy-embedded-in-food rule. Do not say children died (unverified).
- Src: https://www.army.mil/article/123460/fines_can_be_nasty_surprise_for_surprise_egg_mailers ; https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20080917230049/http://www.fda.gov/ora/fiars/ora_import_ia3402.html ; https://reason.com/2017/11/30/kinder-eggs-still-illegal-in-america/

### 2. Soft raw-milk cheese (brie/camembert type, also young raw cheeses)
- Rule: 21 CFR 133.182 (soft-ripened cheeses): if milk unpasteurised, cheese must be cured >=60 days at >=35F. Standards date ~1949-1950 (sources differ on year; say "since the late 1940s"). [SOLID text, DISPUTED year]
- Reason: pathogen (listeria, salmonella, typhoid-era data) safety. [SOLID]
- Allowed: raw-milk cheese aged 60+ days (Parmesan, Gruyere, aged Cheddar); pasteurised cheeses any age. Banned: young raw-milk cheese in interstate sale/import.
- Twist: FDA/Health Canada risk assessment said 60-day rule may not reduce listeria risk and modelling suggested removal would lower risk; FDA 2016 sampling report agrees aging may not eliminate pathogens. American Cheese Society disagrees. [SOLID]
- Caveat: Applies to commerce (interstate/import), many states allow in-state sale. Not literally "illegal to eat". Ukrainian tie-in: farmers' market cheese.
- Src: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-133/subpart-B/section-133.182 ; https://www.fda.gov/files/food/published/FY2014%E2%80%932016-Microbiological-Sampling-Assignment-Summary-Report--Raw-Milk-Cheese-Aged-60-Days.pdf

### 3. Raw ("from under the cow") milk
- Rule: 21 CFR 1240.61 (Aug 10 1987, 52 Fed Reg 29509): no interstate distribution of unpasteurised milk/milk products for direct human consumption. [SOLID]
- Origin: court-ordered. Public Citizen v. Heckler, 653 F.Supp. 1229 (D.D.C. 1986) ordered FDA to ban interstate raw milk. [SOLID]
- Allowed: intrastate sale depends on state; many states allow farm/retail sale, some ban. Pasteurisation shipments to plants are exempt.
- Caveat: federal ban is only interstate; say "cannot cross state lines". Later suits (2010 FTCLDF etc.) dismissed.
- Src: https://archives.federalregister.gov/issue_slice/1987/8/10/29509-29516.pdf ; https://biotech.law.lsu.edu/cases/FDA/heckler-milk.htm

### 4. Sturgeon caviar - BELUGA (Black Sea)
- Action: US Fish & Wildlife Service, 28 Oct 2005 (after 30 Sep 2005 decision): suspended import/re-export of beluga (Huso huso) caviar+meat from Black Sea basin; even personal effects. Ukraine explicitly on country list (with Bulgaria, Georgia, Romania, Russia, Turkey, Serbia-Montenegro). [SOLID]
- Reason: Black Sea states failed to submit beluga conservation laws/management plans by 6 Sep 2005 (Endangered Species Act threatened listing + special rule). [SOLID]
- Allowed: osetra, sevruga, US white sturgeon caviar. Legal-before-2005 stock could be sold domestically for limited time.
- Current status: sales sites say beluga "legal again"/still illegal - conflicting; only Caspian/other beluga origins treated differently since 2007 quotas. [DISPUTED] Say "the US blocked beluga imports from 2005" - past tense, no claim about today.
- Caveat: not red salmon caviar (fine). Don't confuse with CITES 2006 global quota suspension.
- Src: https://www.fws.gov/story/2005-10/service-suspends-trade-threatened-beluga-sturgeon

### 5. Absinthe
- US: 1912 absinthe barred (USDA/Food Inspection era; exact instrument unclear), became sellable again 2007 when TTB approved first brands (Lucid, Kubler) with thujone under 10 ppm (10 mg/kg). TTB Industry Circular 2007-5 (17 Oct 2007) on labelling term "absinthe". [SOLID dates; DISPUTED "formal law"]
- Reason: thujone/hallucination scare (wormwood). Modern testing: trace amounts only. [SOLID]
- Nuance: VinePair says "wasn't really illegal" while also saying "official ban" - mechanism disputed. Say "effectively unavailable from 1912 to 2007".
- Src: https://vinepair.com/wine-blog/why-is-absinthe-legal-now-because-it-wasnt-really-illegal-in-the-first-place ; https://erowid.org/chemicals/absinthe/absinthe_law1.shtml

### 6. Lungs (beef/pork/lamb lungs, "легені", haggis)
- Rule: USDA FSIS 1971: "Livestock lungs shall not be saved for use as human food" (9 CFR 310.16 - number not confirmed in fetched page, verify before showing on screen). [SOLID quote, DISPUTED cite]
- Reason: fluids / stomach contents can get sucked into lungs during slaughter; food-safety. [SOLID]
- Allowed: pet food. Banned: human food. Haggis: sep. 1989 APHIS/USDA ruminant-import rule (scrapie concern) also blocks imports. Jan 2010 report USDA would lift haggis ban - USDA denied; ban remained in 2014. 2021 "lifted" claim unverified [DISPUTED]. Check current status before saying "still".
- Ukrainian tie-in: lungs in kholodets/pies/offal at ordinary markets (confirm in script, user knowledge).
- Src: https://www.vice.com/en/article/the-usda-doesnt-want-us-to-eat-lungs/ ; https://www.foodsafetynews.com/2010/01/usda-to-lift-21-year-ban-on-haggis/

### 7. Home-distilled spirits (samohon / moonshine)
- Law: 26 USC 5042(a)(2), 5053(e), 5178(a)(1)(B) (no still in a dwelling), 5601(a)(6): felony, up to 5 yrs + $10,000 per count. Beer/wine at home allowed for adults, spirits not. [SOLID - ttb.gov]
- Reason: primarily tax revenue/safety (TTB). 
- Twist: 5th Circuit, 10 Apr 2026, docket 24-10760, affirmed district ruling that the home-distilling ban exceeds Congress's taxing/necessary-and-proper powers. Plaintiffs/injunction scope and further appeal not confirmed. [DISPUTED - say "court ruled in 2026 that the ban is unconstitutional; federal law still on books/under challenge", verify status]
- Caveat: a hobby/vivid item; legal status in flux - verify before publishing.
- Src: https://www.ttb.gov/regulated-commodities/beverage-alcohol/distilled-spirits/penalties-for-illegal-distilling ; https://natlawreview.com/article/5th-circuit-affirms-ruling-home-distillation-ban-unconstitutional
